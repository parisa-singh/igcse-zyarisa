A note from me

Why I built this

When I was in Grade 9, I thought being organised was studying. I spent almost six months making colour-coded planners, rewriting my notes to look pretty, and watching "study with me" videos instead of actually studying. It felt productive. It wasn't.

Then someone showed me the one thing that changed everything: the Cambridge syllabus for every subject has a topic checklist built into the back of it — an official list of exactly what you need to know, in Cambridge's own words. I'd been inventing my own revision lists for months when the real one was sitting in a free PDF the whole time. That's the kind of thing this site exists to hand you on day one instead of month six.

The truth is IGCSE isn't about how hard you work — it's about whether you're working on the right things in the right order. Content first, then exam technique. Testing yourself, not re-reading. Two years is genuinely enough time if you don't waste the first one. Everything here is what I'd tell you if we were sitting at the kitchen table. Take what helps, ignore what doesn't, and come back when you're stuck.

— your sister

Do these first

Five things to do this week

  1. Download the syllabus for every subject

    Go to Cambridge's site and get the official syllabus PDF for each of your nine subjects. This is your real curriculum — nothing on the exam is outside it, and nothing outside it is worth your time.

  2. Find the topic checklist at the back of each syllabus

    Most syllabuses have a self-assessment checklist near the end. That's the list you'll turn into your traffic-light tracker. Don't build your own from scratch — Cambridge already did it.

  3. Set up your traffic-light tracker for one subject

    Just one, to start. Pick the subject you find hardest. Rate each topic Red / Amber / Green honestly. The tracker's here.

  4. Bookmark the past-paper sites

    You'll live on these in Grade 10. Get them saved now so they're one click away. They're all on the Resources page.

  5. Read the Past Papers page before you touch a paper

    There's a right way and a wrong way to use past papers, and the wrong way wastes them. Learn the workflow first. Start here.
