French

This one isn't organised by topic — it's organised by skill. You're not marked on how much you know about a theme, you're marked on how well you can listen, read, write, and speak French. Keep that distinction in your head and the whole subject gets clearer.

The subject

What French is, at IGCSE

IGCSE French tests your ability to communicate in French across four skills: listening, reading, writing, and speaking. There's no list of "topics" to revise the way there is in Physics or Business — instead, everyday themes (home, school, holidays, health, the world around us) show up again and again as the material your skills get tested on. Success here comes from two things stacked together: steady, broad vocabulary and grammar built up over time, plus exam technique specific to each skill — because a strong French speaker can still lose marks by not knowing how each paper wants the language delivered.

Paper 4 isn't fixed

Depending on how your school enters you, Paper 4 may be a speaking exam or a coursework component — the two aren't the same preparation. Confirm which one applies to you early, so you're not practising the wrong thing in the months before the exam.

Where marks leak

Skill-based pitfalls in French

Listening — the recording plays twice

Use the first listen to get the gist — topic, speakers, general direction. Use the second listen to answer precisely, filling in details and checking spelling. Trying to write full answers on the first pass means you miss the bigger picture and scramble on the second.

Directed writing — answer every bullet point

The task gives you bullet points to respond to, and each one carries marks. Missing even one — no matter how good your French is everywhere else — loses marks that can't be recovered elsewhere in the answer. Tick them off as you write.

Reading — don't over-translate

You're being tested on comprehension, not translation ability. Answer exactly what's asked, in the language the question requires (English or French) — padding your answer with an unnecessary full translation wastes time and doesn't earn extra marks.

The specific stuff

French tips that actually move marks

Structure your tracker by skill, not topic

Unlike most subjects, your tracker sections here should be listening / reading / writing / speaking — not content units. Rating "verb tenses" as a topic doesn't map onto how you're actually examined.

Master the two-listen strategy

First listen for gist and structure, second listen for precise detail. Practise this rhythm with past papers until it's automatic — don't try to catch everything the first time round.

Cover every bullet, in the right register

Directed writing tasks specify a register (formal letter, informal email, article). Match it, and address every single bullet point — completeness beats elegance here.

Know your speaking format early

Check with your school whether Paper 4 is a speaking exam or coursework, then practise accordingly — the skills you build for a live oral exam differ from those for a coursework portfolio.
