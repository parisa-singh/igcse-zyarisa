The subject

What 0607 is, at IGCSE

Cambridge International Mathematics (0607) covers number, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, statistics and probability — but its personality is different from a standard maths syllabus. A graphical calculator is assumed and central, so questions expect you to use it fluently. And unique among IGCSEs, it includes an investigation paper that assesses mathematical thinking rather than routine calculation. You follow either the Core or Extended route; Extended reaches the top grades.

Unique to 0607

The investigation paper

What it is: an open-ended mathematical investigation. You're given a mathematical scenario and asked to explore it — find patterns, make conjectures, and prove or disprove them. It is not a "solve this" paper. It's "explore this and show your mathematical thinking."

What it tests: mathematical reasoning, communication, pattern recognition, and generalisation. The calculator is allowed and important — use its table and graph functions to explore.

How to practise: past investigation papers are the only real practice material, and there aren't many of them. Work through every one available. The goal isn't to reach "the answer" — it's to show structured mathematical exploration.

The common mistake: treating it like a structured paper and only doing calculations. The marks are for showing your thinking process — your patterns, your conjectures, your reasoning — not just a final number.

Don't forget Papers 1 & 2 exist

Papers 1 and 2 are non-calculator — students who lean entirely on the calculator get caught out. You still need solid mental and written arithmetic and algebra. The Core route sits Papers 1, 3, 5; the Extended route sits Papers 2, 4, 6. Confirm your route with your teacher.

Where marks leak

Command-word pitfalls in 0607

"Show that…"

You must reach the given result with visible working. You can't just state it — the marks are entirely in the steps between.

"Work out" / "Calculate"

Show your method even when the calculator does the arithmetic. On calculator papers, examiners still want to see the setup — the equation, the substitution — not just the answer off the screen.

Investigation: "Investigate" / "Comment"

These reward exploration and generalisation. Describe the pattern, express it algebraically if you can, and say what it means. A bare table of numbers with no commentary scores poorly.

Before you buy

Check your school's calculator policy first. Some schools require or recommend a specific model — ask before spending. Where to buy in India: Amazon.in (search the exact model name), stationery shops near international schools, or the school supplier if there is one.

Both major Casio models have an exam mode that restricts features during exams. You (or the exam centre) must activate it. Here's how, and what it does.

Casio fx-CG50 — entering exam mode

  • Hold F1 + F2 + ON simultaneously
  • Select exam mode from the menu
  • A green LED activates, confirming exam mode is on
  • Stays active for 12 hours, or until manually exited after the exam

Look for the flashing green light — that's how invigilators verify it too.

Casio fx-991EX — entering exam mode

  • Hold ÷ + × + ON simultaneously
  • Follow the on-screen prompts

What gets reset entering exam mode

  • All stored programs deleted
  • All stored variables cleared
  • All user-defined functions removed
  • Custom settings reset to default

What exam mode locks

  • CAS features (if any)
  • Stored user programs
  • Custom settings that could give an unfair advantage
Back up first

Back up anything you want to keep before activating exam mode — it wipes stored programs, variables and settings. Also: ask your school whether the exam centre activates exam mode or expects you to. This varies — some centres do it themselves, some ask students. Know which applies to you.

Night-before-exam checklist

  • ☐ Back up any stored programs/notes you want to keep
  • ☐ Activate exam mode
  • ☐ Confirm the green LED is lit (CG50) or the indicator is showing
  • ☐ Check battery level — replace if below 50%
  • ☐ Pack calculator, backup batteries, and the official manual page if allowed

Deeper than the quick-reference cards: for each major topic, what the calculator can and can't do for you, and the mistake to avoid. Remember — some things must be shown by hand, because the examiner is marking your method, not the screen.

1 · Statistics & data handling

Can do: mean, standard deviation, regression, correlation, and building a summary from a frequency table — fast and reliably. The highest-calculator-use topic in 0607.

Can't do for you: choosing the right statistic, or interpreting what it means in context. Cumulative-frequency curves still need reading by hand.

Common mistake Entering frequencies as separate data points instead of using the frequency column — it throws off the mean.

2 · Graphing & graph analysis

Can do: plot functions, find roots, intersections and turning points, and solve equations graphically. Central to the investigation paper.

Can't do for you: a well-set window. If the graph looks empty, your window is wrong, not the maths.

Common mistake Reading an intersection off a badly-scaled graph instead of using G-Solv for the exact value.

3 · Simultaneous equations

Can do: the equation solver handles linear simultaneous equations directly; graphically, plot both and find the intersection.

Common mistake Using the solver when the question says "solve graphically" — do what the command word asks.

4 · Matrices

Can do: entry, addition, subtraction, multiplication, inverse and determinant.

Common mistake Dimension mismatch — trying to multiply matrices whose sizes don't line up. Check rows × columns before you press equals.

5 · Trigonometry

Can do: all trig and inverse-trig values; solve trig equations graphically.

Common mistake Wrong angle mode. Degrees vs radians is one of the most common sources of wrong answers in the whole subject — check the indicator every time.

6 · The investigation paper

Can do: use the table function to generate values and spot patterns, and the graph to visualise relationships as you explore.

Common mistake Letting the calculator replace your written reasoning. Document the exploration — table, pattern, conjecture, check — in words. The marks live in the thinking you show.

The specific stuff

0607 tips that move marks

Master both hands: calc and non-calc

Practise Papers 1/2 (non-calculator) as their own skill. Fluent mental arithmetic and algebra protect you when the calculator isn't allowed.

Show setup even on calculator papers

Write the equation and the substitution before the answer. Method marks survive a wrong final value; a lone answer off the screen doesn't.

Treat the investigation as a skill, not luck

There's a repeatable structure: tabulate, spot the pattern, conjecture, test, generalise, justify. Practise that loop on every past investigation.

Know your calculator cold before the exam

Fumbling menus wastes time you don't have on 45-minute papers. Drill the quick-reference sequences until they're automatic.
