English Language

Underneath every question on this paper is the same pair of words: audience and purpose. Who are you writing for, and why? Answer that honestly before you write a single sentence, and most of this subject falls into place.

The subject

What English Language is, at IGCSE

IGCSE English Language tests two different skills side by side: how well you read and how well you write. On the reading side, you'll comprehend, summarise, and analyse unfamiliar passages — pulling out meaning, condensing it accurately, and explaining how a writer's language and structure create effect. On the writing side, you produce your own directed and creative pieces, matched precisely to a stated form, audience, and purpose. You're marked on both halves: understanding what's in front of you, and controlling what you put on the page yourself.

Where marks leak

Command-word pitfalls in English Language

Summary

Include only what's relevant to the question, put it in your own words, and stop there. No examples, no quotations lifted from the passage, no repeating the same point twice in different clothes — each of those wastes words and earns nothing.

"How does the writer..." / language analysis

Identify the technique, then explain its effect on the reader. Naming a metaphor or a list of three and stopping there is half an answer — the marks live in the "so what," not the spotting.

Directed writing

Match the required form, audience, and purpose exactly — a speech is not a letter is not an article. Examiners can tell in the first paragraph whether you've registered who you're supposedly writing for.

The specific stuff

English Language tips that actually move marks

Paper 1 Reading: master the summary technique

Know exactly what to pull in (points that answer the question, in your own words) and what to leave out (irrelevant detail, examples, repetition). A tight, accurate summary beats a long one every time.

Paper 2 Writing: directed vs creative/descriptive

Directed writing rewards matching form, audience, and purpose with clear, persuasive or informative control. Creative/descriptive rewards vivid, structured, original language. Know how marks split between content and language for each — they're not weighted the same way.

Audience & purpose — name them first

These are the two things every language question is really about. Before you write a word, say out loud who you're writing for and why. If you can't answer both, you're not ready to start the answer.

Structure and language, not just language

Passages are built as well as written — don't only comment on word choice. Look at how the piece opens, shifts, and closes, and how that structure shapes the reader's experience too.

Note on the 2027 syllabus update

Cambridge updated 0500 for examination from 2027 onward, and you sit your exams in 2028 — so you're in that updated cohort. Whenever you use a specimen paper or past paper to revise, double-check it's from the updated syllabus, not the old one. Practising against the wrong version wastes time and can teach you the wrong format.
