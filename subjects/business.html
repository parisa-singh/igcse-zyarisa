Business Studies

This subject isn't really about how much business theory you can recite — it's about whether you can bend that theory around a case study you've never seen before. Learn to apply, and the marks follow.

The subject

What Business Studies is, at IGCSE

IGCSE Business Studies covers how a business is set up and run — enterprise and business growth, marketing, operations, finance, people, and the external environment it all sits inside. There's a single tier, so everyone sits the same papers and is chasing the same top grade. The theory itself isn't hard, but the exam barely rewards knowing it in the abstract — it rewards applying it to a specific business in a specific situation, and then judging what that business should actually do. It's an application-and-evaluation subject wearing a content subject's syllabus.

Where marks leak

Command-word pitfalls in Business

"Analyse"

Analyse wants a chain of reasoning, not a list of points. "This could increase profit" is a start; "this could increase profit, because X, which means Y, which means Z" is what actually scores. Each link has to be developed, not just stated.

"Evaluate" / "Justify"

Both want a decision — yes or no, this option or that one — and that decision has to follow logically from the argument you just made. "It depends" with nothing to back it up scores nothing. Weigh the points against each other, then commit to an answer.

"Explain"

Explain wants cause leading to effect, and that effect has to be tied back to the specific business in front of you — not business theory floating in space. If your explanation would work for any company on earth, it isn't explaining enough.

The specific stuff

Business tips that actually move marks

Application isn't optional — it's the exam

Every answer needs to be linked back to the case study business by name, using its actual context — its product, its market, its numbers. Generic business knowledge with no link to the case earns you nothing, even if it's technically correct.

Don't reuse the same application point

Application marks are only credited once per question. If you make the same case-study point twice to pad out an answer, the second mention earns nothing — it just wastes time you needed for a second, different point.

Part (e) questions carry no application marks

The final part of a case-study question is usually pure evaluation. Don't burn time hunting for case-study detail to reference here — spend it building and weighing your argument instead.

Evaluation means a decision that follows

Good evaluation isn't a summary of both sides — it's a justified decision that grows out of the analysis you already wrote. State it clearly at the end, and make sure it's the answer your own argument actually points to.

Keyword bank — get the terminology exact

Business terminology is tested precisely, not loosely. "Cash flow" and "profit" are not the same thing and examiners will mark you down for confusing them. "Break-even" has a specific meaning and formula, not a vague sense of "no loss." Motivation theory names — Maslow, Herzberg, Taylor — need to be spelled and attributed correctly. Build a keyword bank per topic and drill it; sloppy vocabulary costs marks even when your reasoning is right.
