The subject

What Psychology is, at IGCSE

IGCSE Psychology introduces you to the scientific study of the mind and behaviour through a set of designated core studies, the research methods psychologists use to investigate people, and the major approaches — biological, cognitive, learning, psychodynamic, and social — that explain why we think and act as we do. A large part of the exam is applied: you're regularly asked to take a psychological concept or study and apply it to a scenario you haven't seen before, rather than simply recall a definition.

Where marks leak

Command-word pitfalls in Psychology

"Describe" a study

Name the researcher, the method, the sample, and the findings — be precise and complete. A vague summary of "what the study was about" without these four pieces loses marks fast.

"Apply" questions

Apply the concept to the given scenario — don't just recall the theory. This is the same trap as in Business: examiners want your answer rooted in the specific context they gave you, not a general definition copied out.

"Evaluate" a study or method

Give strengths and weaknesses of the design — think reliability, validity, sampling, and ethics. A one-sided answer, or one that just restates the method, won't score the evaluation marks.

The specific stuff

Psychology tips that actually move marks

Core studies are heavily tested

The specific studies Cambridge designates come up again and again. Know each one's researcher, method, sample, and findings — and its evaluation. Half-knowing a study is worse than you'd think, because questions probe the detail.

Research methods carry real weight

A significant portion of marks sits here every paper. Understand variables, reliability, validity, and sampling methods properly — not just as vocabulary, but well enough to critique a method you've never seen before.

Application questions need the scenario

Apply psychological concepts to the given scenario, not just the theory in the abstract. Practise reading the scenario twice before you answer — the marks live in the connection you draw.

Use every past paper

The archive is small compared to older subjects, so don't waste any of it doing casual reading-through. Save some papers for proper timed practice once you're in Grade 10.
