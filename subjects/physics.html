The subject

What Physics is, at IGCSE

IGCSE Physics is the study of how the physical world behaves — forces and motion, energy, waves, electricity, magnetism, the atom, and the universe. It rewards precise, structured thinking: define a quantity correctly, apply the right equation, show every step, and attach the right unit. It's less about memorising and more about applying a fairly small set of principles carefully. You'll follow either the Core or Extended route depending on your target grades — Extended unlocks the top grades and includes more demanding papers.

Where marks leak

Command-word pitfalls in Physics

"State" vs "Explain"

"State the relationship" wants one line. "Explain why" wants a cause-effect chain. Physics markers are ruthless about this — an explanation with no "because" usually scores zero.

"Describe" an experiment

Give the method as steps someone could follow: what you change, what you measure, what you keep the same. Vague "I would test it" answers get nothing.

"Calculate" / "Determine"

Write the equation, substitute values, then give the answer with a unit. Method marks exist even when the final number is wrong — so never leave it blank.

The specific stuff

Physics tips that actually move marks

Always include units

Physics mark schemes explicitly require them. A correct number with no unit — or the wrong unit — loses the mark. Make writing the unit part of the same reflex as writing the number.

Show every step of working

Every calculation, every step, even when the answer looks obvious. Method marks are awarded for the working. If the final answer is wrong but the method is right, you still score.

Weight vs mass — don't mix them

Weight is a force, measured in newtons (N). Mass is measured in kilograms (kg). Swapping them is one of the most common errors in examiner reports. W = mg.

Match significant figures

Give your answer to the same number of significant figures as the data in the question, unless told otherwise. Over-precise answers (a screen full of decimals) can lose the mark.

Paper 6 — Alternative to Practical

These questions test experimental skills, not just physics knowledge. Practise the specific skills: plotting graphs accurately, calculating a gradient, identifying anomalous results, reading instruments precisely, and suggesting realistic improvements to a method. You can revise the whole paper by working through past Paper 6s — the question types repeat every year.
