The highest-value practice there is

Past papers, done properly.

Past papers are the closest thing to knowing the questions in advance — but only if you use them right. Done wrong, you burn a limited resource and learn nothing. Here's the whole method.

It depends on your year

How to use past papers — Grade 9 vs Grade 10

As a Grade 9 student

  • Don't do full timed papers yet. You haven't covered all the content.
  • Use papers as a source of topic questions, not practice exams.
  • After a chapter in class: find questions on that chapter from the last 3–4 papers. Do them untimed, notes available, then mark with the scheme.
  • Goal: learn what Cambridge asks about this topic and what language the mark scheme accepts.
  • Keep a log of questions attempted and the topics they covered.

As a Grade 10 student

  • Now you do full timed papers, under real conditions. No notes. No phone. Timer running.
  • Mark the same day with the actual mark scheme — not from memory.
  • Read the examiner report for that session.
  • Log every mark lost and why (the three categories below).
  • Aim for 5–8 full papers per subject before mocks.
  • After mocks, target your weakest papers specifically — don't just re-do the ones you did well on.
The habit that separates top students

The three categories of lost marks

When you mark a paper, don't just note "wrong." Categorise every lost mark — because the fix is completely different for each one. Students who only track "got it wrong" never learn what to change.

Content

Didn't know the topic.

Fix: go back to the syllabus and revise. Move the topic to Red/Amber in your tracker.

Technique

Knew the content, answered the wrong command word (described when asked to explain, etc.).

Fix: re-read the command-words table. Drill that verb specifically.

Wording

Right idea, wrong terminology. The scheme wanted "limiting factor"; you wrote "the thing that slows it down."

Fix: add it to your keyword bank (below).

Do it in this order, every time

The 6-step past-paper workflow

  1. Download paper + mark scheme together

    Same year, same session. Before you start. Never begin without the scheme already downloaded.

  2. Attempt under timed conditions

    Full time. No notes. No phone. Treat it exactly like the real exam — that's the only way it builds exam stamina.

  3. Mark with the scheme, word for word — same day

    Don't self-mark from memory. Read every accepted answer. Circle everything the scheme credits that you didn't write.

  4. Log every lost mark with its category

    Content / Technique / Wording. This log is the most useful revision document you'll own by Grade 10.

  5. Read the examiner report for that session

    Find the questions you lost marks on. See exactly what the examiner said about how students answered them.

  6. Update your traffic-light tracker

    Only move a topic to Green if you answered its questions correctly. Lost marks? It stays Amber or drops to Red. Open the tracker →

Read schemes like an examiner

Mark-scheme symbols, decoded

Mark schemes have their own shorthand. Once you can read it fluently, marking your own work becomes fast and accurate.

SymbolNameWhat it means
/Forward slash"Or" — separates acceptable alternatives. Any one earns the mark. e.g. "increases / rises / goes up".
( )ParenthesesOptional extra detail — earns credit but isn't required for the base mark.
owtteOr words to that effectExact wording doesn't matter — the correct idea in your own words earns the mark. But it must be specific and correct.
e.g.ExampleShows one acceptable answer; other answers fitting the same idea are also accepted unless stated otherwise.
max [n]MaximumYou earn up to n marks from the listed bullets. Writing extra points earns nothing beyond n.
✓TickEach tick = one mark. Count the ticks to know how many distinct points the answer needs.
L1 / L2 / L3Level descriptorsUsed in long-answer questions. Describes the quality of a response, not specific content. Your answer is placed in a band.
AWAlternative wordingSpecifically named alternatives Cambridge will accept. Different from owtte — these are listed exactly.
ECFError carried forwardIn calculations — if an earlier step was wrong but used correctly later, you may still earn method marks.
bodBenefit of the doubtThe examiner may award a borderline response. Not something to rely on.
Straight from the reports

What examiners actually say

These patterns appear in Cambridge IGCSE Principal Examiner Reports across multiple subjects and multiple years. Read them as a list of traps to avoid.

Candidates answered in the wrong style — describing when asked to explain, or explaining when asked to state.Pattern across Sciences, Business, Economics
Responses to 'Suggest' questions were too generic. Candidates applied rote knowledge rather than the specific scenario given.Pattern across Sciences, Humanities
Vague or everyday language was used where syllabus terminology was required. Marks could not be awarded.Pattern across Sciences, Business
Candidates did not show working in calculation questions. When the final answer was incorrect, no method marks could be awarded.Pattern across Sciences, Mathematics
Evaluation questions were answered by restating earlier analysis points rather than reaching a justified conclusion.Pattern across Business, Economics
Candidates wrote far more than necessary for low-mark questions, leaving insufficient time for higher-mark questions.Pattern across multiple subjects
Compare questions were answered by describing A then describing B separately, without any explicit comparison.Pattern across Sciences, Languages
The usual suspects

Where students lose marks — by category

Command-word mismatch most common

Answered the wrong type of question. The word at the start tells you exactly what to do — underline it before writing anything.

Missing cause-effect link for "Explain"

Wrote what happens, not why. "The rate increases" earns nothing. "The rate increases because there are more substrate molecules available to bind to the enzyme active site" earns the mark.

Generic answers for "Suggest"

Ignored the scenario and answered from memory. Suggest questions are always about the specific situation described.

Terminology gaps

Knew the concept, used everyday language. Mark schemes award syllabus-specific terms.

Working not shown

Skipped steps in calculations, then lost method marks when the final answer was wrong.

Units missing or wrong

Correct number, no unit or wrong unit — mark not awarded.

Over-writing low-mark questions

Six lines for a 1-mark question, then ran out of time on the 6-mark ones.

Not answering the number asked

"State two factors" — listed three. Cambridge often marks only the first two.

The keyword bank
